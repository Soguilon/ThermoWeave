Proposed functional apparel concept

Hot Days. Humid Air.
Everyday Discomfort.

“Wear it, and let it breathe.”

ThermoWeave is a proposed functional T-shirt line designed around the hot and humid climate of the Philippines. It explores breathable, stretchable fabric and strategically placed, temperature-responsive ventilation intended to increase airflow when the wearer gets hot.

For students and commuters walking to school, waiting for transportation, or carrying backpacks, clothing comfort is an everyday concern.

What if your everyday T-shirt were designed with airflow in mind? ThermoWeave explores a new approach to everyday apparel through breathable fabric and proposed temperature-responsive ventilation.

The Problem

01

Heat & Humidity

High temperatures and humidity affect thermal comfort. The body relies on sweat evaporation to release heat, and high humidity can make that evaporation less effective.

02

Moisture & Comfort

How moisture moves — or doesn't — against the skin plays a large role in how comfortable clothing feels during a warm, humid day.

03

An Opportunity

Clothing construction, textile properties, and airflow placement can influence comfort — an open area for better everyday apparel design.

Designed Around the Way We Feel Heat

ThermoWeave is a proposed T-shirt concept combining comfortable, stretchable fabric with strategically positioned ventilation — intended for everyday wear in warm, humid conditions, offered in two proposed price tiers.

01

Breathable Fabric

Textile structure influences how air and water vapor move through fabric. Suitable material selection may help improve comfort and moisture management.

02

Targeted Ventilation

Ventilation openings may improve airflow around areas where heat and moisture accumulate. ThermoWeave proposes strategically designed ventilation to support comfort.

03

Proposed Thermoresponsive Adaptation

Some engineered materials respond to temperature or other environmental changes. ThermoWeave proposes exploring a mechanism that may adjust ventilation as conditions change.

ThermoWeave is a product concept under development. Its specific materials, temperature-responsive behavior, comfort benefits, and durability require prototype development and testing.

Brand Theme & Product Purpose

ThermoWeave — “Wear it, and let it breathe.”

ThermoWeave explores how fabric selection, moisture management, airflow, and heat transfer could improve everyday clothing comfort in the Philippines. The concept combines a familiar T-shirt design with proposed ventilation features that may respond to changing temperature conditions.

The scientific rationale is grounded in textile research showing that fabric structure, moisture movement, airflow, and heat transfer influence clothing comfort. The specific ThermoWeave design and its performance are not yet validated; they must be evaluated through prototype development and controlled testing.

PAGASA describes the Philippines as having a tropical maritime climate. The supplied background identifies a mean annual temperature of 26.6°C. These climate conditions provide context for the concept, but do not by themselves prove that ThermoWeave will improve comfort.

Costing & Commercial Potential

Proposed selling prices: Student Edition ₱399 · Premium Edition ₱599 · Price difference ₱200.

The editions are intended to share the same overall design, color family, silhouette, collar, sleeves, and general construction. The Premium Edition's proposed price difference reflects intended higher-grade fabric, enhanced ventilation, and improved hot/cold adaptability—not a different shirt design.

Pricing must be validated. Obtain supplier quotations for fabric, ventilation materials, garment construction, labor, packaging, transport, and quality testing. Customer surveys, interviews, and pre-orders should also test whether the target market considers the prices reasonable.

Gross profit per shirt is the selling price minus the verified unit production cost. This is not net profit; operating expenses such as marketing, delivery, sampling, testing, and administration must also be considered.

Target Market & Sales Strategy

01 · Audience

Students & Commuters

The initial audience includes classmates, other students, commuters, and people who regularly spend time outdoors in warm, humid conditions. Campus activities, walking, public transportation, and backpack use are relevant everyday scenarios to explore.

02 · Channels

Low-Cost Launch

Begin with school-based promotion, social media, product photographs, fabric-detail images, and online pre-orders. Explain the proposed ventilation concept and clearly compare both editions so customers can make informed decisions.

03 · Validation

Learn Before Scaling

Survey potential customers about preferred sizes, fit, acceptable prices, and edition interest. Use pre-orders to estimate demand, then gather user feedback on comfort, breathability, durability, washing performance, and satisfaction before producing larger quantities.

This is a proposed sales approach, not evidence of established demand. Demand must be confirmed through actual survey results, pre-orders, and sales.

Starting Resources & Preliminary Capital

Preliminary starting-capital target: ₱30,000. This is an initial planning assumption, not a verified budget. It should support feasibility work and product validation; it should not be treated as proof that full-scale production is affordable.

People & Expertise

Seek support from textile specialists, garment manufacturers, product designers, testing facilities, and people with marketing and sales experience.

Market Research

Use competitor research, customer interviews, surveys, size and fit preferences, and pre-order trials to assess demand before committing to a larger batch.

A simple cost check shows the challenge: 50 shirts at an assumed ₱550 each would use ₱27,500, leaving only ₱2,500 from ₱30,000 for prototype work, testing, packaging, marketing, and other expenses. Research Basis & Validation

The concept is informed by the relationship between tropical climate, humidity, fabric properties, moisture movement, airflow, and heat transfer. These principles explain why textile selection and garment construction are relevant areas to investigate; they do not establish that the proposed product already works.

  • PAGASA — Climate of the Philippines, for general climate context.
  • Zhang et al. (2024) — Advancements and challenges in thermoregulating textiles: Smart clothing for enhanced personal thermal management, for background on thermoregulating textile research.

Confirm the complete bibliographic details and consult the original publications when preparing a formal academic paper or business proposal. ThermoWeave's materials, responsive behavior, comfort benefits, and durability remain proposed features requiring testing.
